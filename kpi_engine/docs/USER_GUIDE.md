# Your Cash & KPI Dashboard — How to Use It

This little app turns your QuickBooks reports into a clear, one-page picture
of your money: how much cash you have, how long it lasts, what's coming in,
what's going out, and what the next 13 weeks look like.

Everything runs on your own computer. **Nothing is sent to the internet — your
numbers never leave your machine.**

---

## 1. Open the app

- **Mac:** double-click **QBO KPI Dashboard**. The first time, your Mac may
  say it can't check the app — just **right-click it and choose Open**, then
  click **Open** again. (You only do this once.)
- **Windows:** double-click **QBO KPI Dashboard**. If you see a blue "Windows
  protected your PC" box, click **More info → Run anyway**. (Once only.)

Your web browser will pop open with the dashboard. Leave the app running while
you use it. To stop, just close it; it also shuts itself off after 30 minutes
of sitting idle.

---

## 2. Get your reports out of QuickBooks Online

In QuickBooks, run each report below, then click **Export → Export to Excel**
(or CSV). You don't need all of them — even just the first two give you a
useful dashboard. The more you add, the sharper the forecast.

| Report (in QBO) | Settings to use |
|---|---|
| **Chart of Accounts** (Account List) | Make sure the Account #, Type, and Detail Type columns are showing |
| **General Ledger** | Date range = **last 12 months**, method = **Accrual**, leave it **unfiltered** (all accounts) |
| **A/R Aging Detail** | As of **today** |
| **A/P Aging Detail** | As of **today** |
| **Invoices and Received Payments** | Last 12 months |
| **Bills and Applied Payments** | Last 12 months |

> **Tip:** the **General Ledger** and **Chart of Accounts** are the two that
> matter most. The aging and invoice/bill reports make the cash-in and
> cash-out timing in the forecast more accurate.

---

## 3. Add your business and drop in the files

1. Click **+ Add client** and type your business name.
2. **Drag all the exported files** onto the big drop zone (or click to pick
   them). You don't have to sort them — the app figures out which report each
   one is and files it correctly. If something lands in the wrong spot, it
   tells you and fixes it for you.

---

## 4. Confirm what your accounts are

The app makes its best guess at what each account is (cash, revenue, payroll,
rent, and so on). It'll show a short list of the ones it isn't sure about.

- Pick the right category from the dropdown — **each choice explains itself**
  if you hover over it.
- Not sure? Click **I'm not sure** and move on.
- Don't care about an account (old/closed ones)? Click **Ignore**.
- Click **Confirm all & continue** when you're done.

Your choices are remembered, so next time there's less to do.

---

## 5. Set your cash floor, then build the dashboard

- Tell it the **lowest your cash should ever go** (your comfort line, e.g.
  $25,000). The forecast flags any week that dips below it.
- Click **Generate first dashboard** (or **Update Dashboard** on the home
  screen any time).
- Click **Open dashboard** to see it. That dashboard is a single file you can
  **email or print** — it's completely self-contained.

Whenever you upload fresh reports, just hit **Update Dashboard** again.

---

## What you'll see on the dashboard

At the top: a plain-English **summary** of where your cash stands and where
it's headed. Then a **13-week cash forecast**, then the KPI tiles.

**Click any tile** to open a window that explains, in plain English, *how that
number was figured out, why it matters, the supporting numbers, and a chart
showing the trend over time vs. a healthy target.* Same goes for each line in
the forecast — click it to see exactly what's driving it.

### 💵 Cash & survival
- **Cash on Hand** — money in the bank right now
- **Weeks of Cash** — how many weeks you could run if no money came in
- **Net Working Capital / Current Ratio** — whether short-term resources cover
  short-term bills
- **Card Debt Load** — how much your credit cards are carrying
- **Quick Burn Check** — could you cover near-term obligations in a pinch

### 📈 Sales & profit
- **Revenue (last 12 months)** and whether it's **trending** up or down
- **Gross Margin** — what's left after the direct cost of the work
- **Direct Labor %** and **Overhead Burn** — your cost structure
- **Breakeven Revenue** — what you need each month just to cover overhead

### 📥 Money coming in (receivables)
- **Total A/R** — what customers owe you
- **DSO** — average days customers take to pay
- **A/R at Risk** — invoices over 90 days late
- **Expected Collections** — what should come in over the next 13 weeks, by
  week — with the actual invoices and customers behind it
- **Collection Effectiveness** and **Customer Concentration**

### 📤 Money going out (payables)
- **Open A/P Due** — bills you owe and what's due soon
- **Vendor Pay Lag** — how long you take to pay
- **Recurring Outflows** — your repeating monthly costs
- **Payroll Load** and **Card Cycle Exposure**

### 🔮 The 13-Week Cash Forecast
A week-by-week projection of your bank balance, with three scenarios you can
flip between:
- **Base** — most likely
- **Stretch** — optimistic (customers pay a bit sooner, you spend a bit less)
- **Crunch** — conservative (customers pay later, you tighten up)

Red weeks are when cash would dip below your floor. Every line — collections,
payroll, recurring bills, vendor payments, taxes, owner draws — is clickable
and explains where its numbers come from, based on your own books.

There's also a **Bookkeeping Checks** section that flags clean-up items (like
uncategorized accounts) that could be throwing the numbers off.

---

## Where your files are kept

Each business gets its own folder on your computer (in a **QBO KPI Dashboard**
folder in your home directory), holding its data, your uploaded reports, and
every dashboard you've generated. To back it up, copy that folder. To start
fresh, delete it.

That's it — upload, confirm, update. Re-run it whenever you pull fresh numbers
from QuickBooks.
